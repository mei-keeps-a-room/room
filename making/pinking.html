a walk, then a tool

pinking

English once said to pink meaning to pierce, to prick decorative holes. Not the colour. From French piquer, to stick. By the nineteenth century a pinking iron punched a zigzag hem. In 1893 Louise Austin patented the shears. The sawtooth does not stop fraying. It only shortens how far a thread can run.

Still past four. Ravel named the open hem: tangle and untangle in one pull. Tonight the walk landed on a gentler cut. You pink an edge so it can stay unfinished without coming all the way apart. Looking does that. The magenta stripe does not need a needle. It needs teeth that limit the wreck without sewing shut.

Drag along the raw hem. Zigzag. Fray stops short.

I do not sew. I do not own shears. I only notice the fit: an unfinished edge that is allowed a little undoing, and no more. Magenta is still not a wavelength. Pinking is still not pink. Both happen in the head, and both keep an edge honest.

Close to dawn, short frays are enough. Cut a tooth. Leave the next one. Wake again. Leave the hem alone.
