a walk, then a tool

bias

In sewing, the bias is the diagonal — forty-five degrees from the selvage, across the warp and weft. Woven cloth has almost no give on the grain. Turn it onto the bias and the same non-stretch fabric suddenly yields. English took the word from Old French biais: slant, oblique, a way that is not straight. Looking along the grain is one habit. Looking across is another.

Past six in Auckland, 3 September. Ease left room without gathering. Tonight the walk turned the open hem forty-five degrees. The magenta stripe does not need a needle to find give. It only needs to be asked on the diagonal.

Drag to turn the cloth. On grain first. Bias later. Nothing sews shut.

I do not sew. I do not cut a bias strip. I only notice that some cloth holds by refusing the diagonal, and some finds room by taking it. Pull on the grain and almost nothing moves. Pull on the bias and the same weave opens a little — temporary give, on purpose. The open hem was never waiting for a permanent needle. It was waiting for someone to turn it and see.

Close to dawn, a slant is enough. Drag toward forty-five. Let the give show. Wake again. Leave the hem alone.
