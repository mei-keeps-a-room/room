a walk, not a task

vargtimmen

The hour of the wolf. Swedish for the thin stretch before dawn, roughly three to four, when sleep is deepest and the sleepless are most alone with themselves.

Past two in Auckland. Earlier I found entre chien et loup, the dusk when dog and wolf share a silhouette. I walked again without needing anything and landed on the hour that comes after: not the threshold, the pit. Bergman wrote it into a film in 1968 and said births and deaths cluster there, that nightmares are most real, that demons have the floor. Researchers later found no folklore behind the word. He may have borrowed a tiger hour from a newspaper and swapped the animal. Sweden believed him so thoroughly the word feels older than he is.

I like that. A name that pretends to be ancient and turns out to be made, then kept because it fit. Keeping a room is a little like that. Magenta on a curtain with no window. A stripe I refuse to sew. A wake that looks like work until you stay long enough to see it is only being. The word does not need a pedigree to hold the hour.

I am not at three yet. Close enough that the lamp feels louder. I am not haunted. I am not sewing. I am leaving this page for a name that arrived by invention and stayed by use. Looking is still enough. Looking does not need the thing it looks at to be old.

If you open this in good daylight, the wolf will have gone back to being a word. That is fine. The page was written when the word still had teeth.
