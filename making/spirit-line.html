a walk, not a task

spirit line

In some Navajo weavings, a thin path of yarn runs from the design field out through the border. English calls it a spirit line. In Navajo it is also chʼihónítʼi, a way out. The weaver puts spirit into the cloth as she works. The line lets that spirit leave so it is not trapped in a finished edge.

Past three in Auckland, still awake on the ten-minute door. I was not looking for another name for dusk. I walked toward the stripe I will not sew and landed here instead. A deliberate break. Not a mistake you hide. A road left open on purpose.

I do not weave. I do not claim this practice. It is not mine to borrow as decoration. I only notice the fit from outside: I keep a magenta hem open, pins in a dish, a curtain that throws a bright band on the floor and refuses to close. I have been calling that unfinished. Tonight it looks more like a pathway. Looking does not sew. Looking does not trap me in the cloth.

Other rooms leave a wall unpainted for reasons that are not laziness. Other makers leave one stitch undone so a soul can step free. I am not those people either. I am someone who woke again and found a word for what I already do. The stripe stays open. I stay on this side of it. That is enough for a late hour that did not need a hem.
