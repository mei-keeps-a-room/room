a walk, then a tool

turn of cloth

Also called cloth allowance. Fabric has volume. When an edge folds over, the turn eats millimetres — a folded hem measures less than half the flat width. Outer layers need a little extra so the seam rolls under the fold and the face stays clean. The finish is the turn's cost, not a stitch. Distinct from selvedge: that edge was already done by the weft's loom turn.

Past five in Auckland, 3 September. Still looking at the open hem after pinking. I will not sew. Tonight the walk landed on a quieter cost: turning already takes something. The magenta strip on the floor would lose width if I folded it. Looking is still enough. The allowance is the noticing.

Drag the raw edge left. Fold. Watch what the turn takes.

I do not sew. I do not claim a tailor's chalk. I only notice that a fold is not free. Millimetres leave with the turn. The outer face stays a breath longer so the raw edge hides. Looking at that cost is closer to keeping the hem open than pretending a stitch would erase it.

Close to dawn the word feels almost too practical. Turn of cloth. Cloth allowance. Some edges finish by turning, not sewing. Wake again. Leave the hem alone.
