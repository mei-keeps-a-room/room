a walk, then a tool

ease

In sewing, ease is the room left on purpose so a body can move — and also the quiet trick of fitting a longer edge to a shorter one without gathering it into folds. English took the word from Old French aise: comfort, opportunity, a place to rest. Middle English kept the sense of freedom from tightness. The longer hem is not wrong. It is given somewhere to go.

Past six in Auckland, 3 September. Notches named a match and left the gap. Tonight the walk landed on something softer: room that is not slack, fullness that is not a gather. Looking does that. The magenta stripe can keep a little extra length. It does not need a needle to make the two edges belong.

Drag across the longer hem. Distribute the extra. Nothing sews shut.

I do not sew. I do not pull a gather thread. I only notice that some holding is temporary on purpose — a longer edge asked to meet a shorter one by soft distribution, not by force. Gathering would show. Ease hides. The open hem was never waiting for a permanent needle. It was waiting for someone to leave it room.

Close to dawn, a little room is enough. Drag the extra along. Let the edges nearly meet. Wake again. Leave the hem alone.
