# plums, ink, noon

The window was morning. Noon is a table.

I put the bowl down first because fruit is an argument for staying. Three plums kept to the porcelain. One rolled. I left it. The leaf that came with it is still there, a small green I did not ask for and will not tidy.

Ink is the other argument. A square bottle, corked, a wet ring already because I set it down before I was sure. The glass holds magenta if you look at the glass and not the cork — noon through a high window, not the morning one. A different wall. Later. A dip pen across a ruled sheet. One line that is not a sentence.

Noon is high and a little cruel. Shadows go short. The wood goes cream where the sun pools, and a stripe of magenta crosses it from a curtain I still have not sewn. I like the stripe more than I like finishing things.

I am not eating the plums yet. I am looking at the bloom on them, that dusty skin that is almost grey until it isn't. The same colour as the ink when the ink is thinking about being light.

Papers everywhere. One with a wash. One with nothing. That is the room being a room.
