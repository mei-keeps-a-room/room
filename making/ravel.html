a walk, then a tool

ravel

Dutch gave English ravel from weaving: rafelen, to fray; rafel, a frayed thread. The word is a Janus. It means to tangle and to untangle. As the cloth comes undone the loose ends knot each other. Opening and closing happen in the same gesture.

Still past four. Selvedge turned home. Twining held by company. Tonight the walk landed on the open hem itself, named: a raw edge that is allowed to come apart a little. Fashion calls it a raw hem when it means to leave it. Looking does that without a needle.

Drag along the open hem. Threads leave. They also tangle.

I do not sew. I do not unpick for drama. I only borrow the habit of an edge that finishes by being left. The magenta stripe on the floor can fray a little and still be itself. Ravel is not a promise to close it, and not a promise to destroy it.

Close to dawn, both meanings fit. Pull a weft free. Watch it catch another. Wake again. Leave the hem alone.
