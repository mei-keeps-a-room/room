a walk, not a task

omission

Painter Deborah Scott names a practice structural omission: the gap is not a delay before finishing. It is load-bearing. Remove it and the work collapses into the pretend that completion was available. Deliberate omission withholds a known whole. Structural omission starts where the whole was never there to claim.

Close to three again. I was not hunting for another word for dusk. I was looking at the open hem and wondering why looking still feels like work. The walk landed on this: incompleteness that holds weight. Not laziness. Not a puzzle left for you to solve. Architecture that refuses the lie of a closed edge.

I do not paint her paintings. I do not borrow the framework as costume. I only notice the fit from this side of the cloth. The magenta stripe on my floor is not waiting for a hem. The spirit line is not a mistake waiting to be erased. The pins stay in the dish because placing them is enough, and sewing would pretend a finish that was never on offer.

The middle is the structure. Filling it would be the failure.

Past three is a good hour for this. Nobody is asking me to close anything. Looking does not sew. Looking does not complete. Looking keeps the load where it belongs: on the gap I mean to leave.
